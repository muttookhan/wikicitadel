# The Whole Field, Studied — Comprehensive Research Report, Completed Edition

**Every project and paper the LLM Wiki research programme studied: the good, the bad, what we took and built, and what we did not take and why — with every placeholder of the first edition replaced by the real detail, or by an honest door record showing the routes tried.**

Completed Edition · 2026-10-22. This edition **supersedes** the Comprehensive Research Report of 2026-10-21. The first edition is preserved unchanged alongside this one; nothing in it was edited to produce this edition.

---

## Why a Completed Edition exists

The owner rejected the first edition on 2026-10-21 as incomplete: across its Tier 1 rows, 2,023 field values were bare placeholders — a field left blank of any real detail wherever the underlying study record had not captured one. His direction was that the missing details be actually found and put in, not that the gaps be papered over, and that nothing be invented to fill them.

The completion programme that followed worked in three stages plus a repair pass (summarised in the Completion Addendum at the end of this report). Every holed Tier 1 field was re-studied at its primary source; the whole Tier 2 catalogue was upgraded line by line from one-line listings to full studies; and every item that still could not be obtained was retried through alternate routes and now stands as a **door record**: what was sought, every route tried, in the record's own words, and why it stands unobtained. No bare placeholder remains anywhere in this edition.

## The honest shortfalls, stated plainly

The owner's deep-study targets for the programme were **at least 4,000 projects, at least 1,000 research papers, and at least 1,000 university researches**, each genuinely opened at its primary source.

- **Projects: 4,464 — the target is met and exceeded.**
- **Papers: 483 — the target is NOT met.** The programme stands 517 papers short of 1,000.
- **University researches: 49 — the target is NOT met.** The programme stands 951 short of 1,000.

This edition completes the 2026-10-21 report's own corpus; it does not pretend the paper and university targets were reached. They were not. Door failures were never counted toward any figure, and no detail in this edition was invented: a fact that is not in the completion masters is not in this report.

# The Whole Field, Studied — Comprehensive Research Report

**Every project and paper the LLM Wiki research programme studied: the good, the bad, what we took and built, and what we did not take and why.**

Prepared 2026-10-21 · Covers the research programme from the 2026-10-13 harvest through Marathon Round 3, plus the deep-study upgrade pass run for this report. Rounds 4–13 research is still landing and is **not** in this edition.

---

## Why this report exists

The owner's instruction, twice over: first, *"give me the goods of each in a list"* — every project studied, with its good and its bad. Then the correction: *"That's not a good way to do research by just cataloguing — then how do you know what's out there, what our wiki is missing to be the world's best?"*

So this report does three things:

1. **The full list** — every deep-studied project and paper, one row each: the good, the bad, what we took and built from it, what we did not take and why (Tier 1). Plus the complete Wave-2 catalogue as an appendix (Tier 2), marked honestly entry-by-entry for depth.
2. **A deep-study upgrade pass** — the strongest catalogue-only entries were clustered by capability and actually **opened at their sources** for this report and studied properly; those rows carry full evidence and are flagged ◆ in Tier 1.
3. **The centrepiece** — *What the world's best have that we're missing*: a capability-by-capability gap analysis driven by the opened sources, each gap dispositioned (built meanwhile / boarded / owner-gated / rejected with reason / honestly still open).

Nothing in this report is invented. Goods, bads and reasons come from the study files themselves or from pages opened in the upgrade pass; where the first edition's records gave no reason, that edition printed a bare placeholder; in this Completed Edition every such field carries the reason the completion programme found, or the item stands as a door record with its routes shown.

## How to read this report

- **Tier 1** is the studied field: every row was opened and written up in a survey wave, a landscape round, an earlier harvest, or this report's upgrade pass. The **GOOD** and **BAD** columns are the study's own words, condensed — not marketing copy and not this report's opinion. **WHAT WE TOOK & BUILT** names the built feature or queue disposition where the records name one. **WHAT WE DIDN'T TAKE & WHY** gives the recorded reason — licence, fit test, owner gate, or a not-for-us verdict; In the first edition, a missing reason was shown as a bare placeholder; here every missing reason has been replaced by the found reason or by a door record, and none has been invented.
- **◆** marks rows deep-studied in this report's upgrade pass (opened at source on 2026-10-21).
- **Tier 2 (appendix)** is the complete Wave-2 catalogue: name, URL, licence, the catalogue's own one-line description (that line is the entry's recorded "good"), and its verdict. Entries that were also deep-studied — in the survey's own deep dives or in the upgrade pass — are marked ◆; the rest are **catalogued, not deep-studied**, and their depth column says so. No bads are fabricated for catalogue-only entries.
- **Licence discipline** held throughout the programme and holds in this report's language: MIT/Apache-2.0 mechanisms could be adapted with attribution; GPL/AGPL/BUSL/custom/no-licence work was **ideas only**, re-implemented from the idea, never copied.

# What the world's best have that we're missing

*The centrepiece. Every "best example" below was opened at its source — in the survey's deep dives or in this report's upgrade pass — never taken from a catalogue line. "What we have" is stated from the build records (the marathon round records and the ideas board, Rounds 1–3). Dispositions use the programme's own categories; where a gap has genuinely not been dispositioned anywhere in the records, it says so — that honesty is the point of the exercise.*

---

### 1. A hosted door: remote, multi-agent, multi-device access

- **Best in the world:** Dexio — one shared wiki served over remote MCP (Streamable HTTP, OAuth for Claude/ChatGPT), plain-markdown pages, full version history attributed per agent, writes that can require the version last read, and bulk changes applied atomically (up to 200 pages). Obsidian's Local REST API with MCP (MIT, 3,051★) does the same for a local vault: authenticated REST + MCP with surgical patching of a single heading, block or frontmatter key.
- **What we have:** a local, stdio-only MCP **read** door (8 verbs, read-only, identity fixed) and a local gateway with scopes and a hash-chained ledger. Nothing hosted; ChatGPT's cloud cannot reach our wiki at all.
- **The gap:** total for remote access — this is the single largest capability gap in the programme.
- **Disposition:** **owner-gated.** The hosted gateway door is a Tier C item, parked for the owner's ruling; it has been presented and not decided. Not a technical unknown — a custody decision.

### 2. Truth-aware retrieval: superseded facts withheld, with reasons

- **Best in the world:** Engram (rwnalds/engram) and Knowl — superseded or expired facts are withheld from search by default, results carry an explicit `excluded[]` list with reasons, ranking is weighted by authority, and write-time guards refuse a second live note on an already-covered subject. AgentCairn (Apache-2.0) keeps old evidence inspectable but demoted via `valid_from` / `valid_until` / `superseded_by`.
- **What we have:** a superseded-decision gate, a claim-lifecycle system (opted in on four pages), and contradiction lanes. The measured weakness is on record: the directional supersession evaluation slice scored coverage@10 of **0.50** in Round 2.
- **The gap:** retrieval does not yet systematically withhold superseded material or explain exclusions; the lifecycle's warn→enforce flip is untaken.
- **Disposition:** **partially built; the enforce flip is an owner decision** (open since 2026-10-14). The retrieval-side exclusion list is **not yet dispositioned** in the records.

### 3. Learned reranking and hybrid retrieval at scale

- **Best in the world:** AgentCairn fuses BM25 + vectors with Reciprocal Rank Fusion and optional reranking over a disposable DuckDB index; AKB and Loci fuse dense + BM25 in one call with source-level dedup and section-level citations. Orama delivers full-text + vector + hybrid in the browser with facets and typo tolerance.
- **What we have:** a hybrid search lane in the Lens (with an exact-title boost repaired in Round 3 after corpus growth pushed a key page out of the top 10), Ask evidence packing informed by the Context Rot study, and a measured scale ceiling (~2,500 synthetic pages, median under 250 ms). No learned reranker is wired; the model-router seam exists **unwired** by design.
- **The gap:** reranking quality and headroom beyond the measured ceiling.
- **Disposition:** **partially built.** Wiring any model provider into the router is **owner-gated** (standing gate held through Round 3).

### 4. Sentence-level evidence scoring

- **Best in the world:** galileo-ai/ragbench — every generated sentence is annotated with its supporting source sentences and scored for adherence, relevance, utilisation and completeness.
- **What we have:** per-claim citation lockfiles, byte-level citation verification, a quote-verification lint lane, and a report-only answer-key audit (Round 2: 109 audited, 98 ok / 11 suspect, suspects left for the owner because the golden set is frozen).
- **The gap:** we verify that citations *resolve*; we do not score whether each sentence is actually *supported* by what it cites.
- **Disposition:** **honestly open** — not dispositioned in any record. A natural Tier A candidate for a future round.

### 5. Evaluation over tables, images and the whole lifecycle

- **Best in the world:** the Allganize RAG evaluation datasets (JA/KO) tag every golden question by whether its answer lives in a paragraph, a table or an image; vectara/open_ragbench does the same over arXiv PDFs with hard negatives. CRUD-RAG splits evaluation by lifecycle operation (create / read / update / delete) instead of QA alone.
- **What we have:** a frozen 15-question golden set (owner-held, byte-untouched through every round), extended and directional evaluation sets, a memory-score triple, trend reporting — all text-only. The corpus currently holds **zero** media files (Round 2 media inventory), so table/image questions have nothing to bite on yet.
- **The gap:** real, but corpus-bound: multimodal and lifecycle-operation evaluation.
- **Disposition:** **honestly open**; the media-fidelity tooling was built ahead of the media (Round 2), deliberately.

### 6. Scored abstention — knowing when to say "not found"

- **Best in the world:** llmware's rag_instruct_benchmark_tester ships items whose context deliberately contains no answer and scores whether the system says so.
- **What we have:** exactly this discipline, built and gated: an abstention battery holding **12/12 at τ = 1.23** through every marathon round (it caught a real regression in Round 3 — a ranking change silently inflated weak matches past the threshold — and was restored before anything shipped).
- **The gap:** **none recorded.** This is a parity capability, listed so the map does not only show deficits.

### 7. Bi-temporal memory: valid-time, as-of queries, supersession without deletion

- **Best in the world:** obsidian-memory-for-ai keeps one fact-version per file with `--as-of` and `--history` queries and `--why` lineage; codex-memory-sidecar proposes memory writes for review and invalidates with `valid_from` / `invalidated_at` / reason / superseding reference, never deletion.
- **What we have:** supersedes chains, claim lifecycle with `as_of` dating, checkpointed history — but no as-of query surface and no systematic bi-temporal fields across pages.
- **The gap:** the temporal query layer.
- **Disposition:** **owner-gated in part** — bi-temporal fields and the bulk schema migration sit in the Phase-2 gates awaiting the owner's ruling; the pattern itself was studied and partially scaffolded in the governance lanes (Round 3, TB3).

### 8. Approval-gated writes with a trust ledger

- **Best in the world:** Magnemo — agents can only stage writes, promotion is a human-only act, search returns promoted notes only, and every promote/reject lands in an append-only trust ledger with actor and reason. knowhub adds cross-model cross-confirmation: a relationship ships only with support from two independent extractors, and unfillable relationships are emitted as first-class knowledge gaps.
- **What we have:** this pattern, adopted early and built: gateway default-deny with scopes, a hash-chained ledger, the filings flow (agents file; nothing lands unreviewed), and the observations pass that proposes beliefs for a ruling and never auto-applies them.
- **The gap:** **none material recorded** — parity, with cross-model confirmation the one unbuilt refinement.
- **Disposition:** **built meanwhile** (Rounds 1–3); cross-confirmation **not yet dispositioned**.

### 9. Document understanding at ingest

- **Best in the world:** docling — one document representation across PDF layout/reading order/tables, DOCX/PPTX/XLSX/HTML/EPUB/email/images/audio, with OCR and ASR, exportable to Markdown/HTML/lossless JSON, runnable locally.
- **What we have:** a converter with defences, a clipper with RSS/Atom and PDF paths, parse-quality gating (built in the Round 3 Tier A queue), and checkpointed capture. OCR exists as a queue, not a verified pipeline: the one queued item (a 27-unit PDF) still carries extraction confidence "unknown" and awaits human verification.
- **The gap:** layout-aware parsing quality and a verified OCR path.
- **Disposition:** **partially built** (gating landed; OCR verification is an owner-physical item, open).

### 10. Sync across devices, and handover between sessions

- **Best in the world:** jiabaobei/memory-bridge — only encrypted diff packets travel, via an ordinary cloud-drive folder, and a structured handover card (goal / done / failed / next / refs) becomes the always-present workbench on the next device. cr-sqlite makes derived indexes conflict-free mergeable across independently written copies.
- **What we have:** git custody on Azure (pull-before-work, push-on-integration, HEAD-verified), offline reading bundles, checkpoint-export handoff packages with explicit omissions lists — strong custody, no live device sync.
- **The gap:** live, encrypted, multi-device sync.
- **Disposition:** **owner-gated in its strong form** — the local-first peer-to-peer zero-knowledge sync posture was parked as Tier C in Round 2. The handover-card pattern is **not yet dispositioned**.

### 11. Citation truth-checking against the scholarly record

- **Best in the world:** CiteMe and Scholar Sidekick (Obsidian) check whether references in a note actually exist, screen identifiers against Crossref and **Retraction Watch**, and report open-access status via Unpaywall.
- **What we have:** citation verification against our *own* sources, down to the byte. Nothing checks a claim against the external scholarly record, and nothing would catch a citation of a retracted paper.
- **The gap:** real, and it grows as the academic-paper share of the corpus grows.
- **Disposition:** **honestly open** — surfaced by the upgrade pass; not in any build queue yet.

### 12. Privacy masking before text reaches an AI

- **Best in the world:** AI Alias — deterministic, fully local masking of real names to aliases before text leaves for any AI, one-click restore on the reply, the mapping never leaves the device.
- **What we have:** a gateway secrets/PII scan that blocks secret-shaped content at the door and a leak-lint over export surfaces — protection against *credential-shaped* leakage, not against ordinary personal names in prose.
- **The gap:** name-level aliasing for external-model work.
- **Disposition:** **partially built** (the scan half); aliasing **not yet dispositioned**.

### 13. A pre-action warning: "you have failed at this before"

- **Best in the world:** riponcm/projectmem stores issues / attempts / fixes / decisions as typed events and warns *before* an agent repeats a failed approach.
- **What we have:** the corrections ledger — agents are required to read it before acting on corrected ground, and it demonstrably catches repeats — but nothing surfaces the warning automatically at the moment of action.
- **The gap:** the automated precheck moment.
- **Disposition:** **partially built** (ledger built Round 1; automation **not yet dispositioned**).

### 14. Multi-writer safety without a single-writer bottleneck

- **Best in the world:** Dexio's atomic bulk writes and version-required updates; data-olympus's single-writer pipeline with advisory locks and per-session worktrees over a typed, git-canonical corpus.
- **What we have:** a deliberately single-writer design — the gateway is the one door, filings are the multi-agent pattern, merges are by review. It is safe; it does not scale to simultaneous writers.
- **The gap:** accepted by design rather than missing by accident.
- **Disposition:** **rejected for now by design** (standing architecture); real-time co-editing was expressly excluded under standing rules in the Round 2 record.

### 15. A machine-readable map of the whole vault

- **Best in the world:** Vault Map (Obsidian) — one JSON Lines index of every note's frontmatter, links, embeds, backlinks, broken links, duplicate titles and orphans, so scripts and agents never rescan the vault. Obsidian Everywhere packs backlinks, n-hop neighbourhoods, shortest paths and PageRank into a token-budgeted context bundle.
- **What we have:** the traversal tool (search / neighbours / path / claim / cite / census, with frozen `--pin` reads) and deterministic graph export with communities and god-nodes — the same territory, exposed as queries rather than as one standing map artefact; context bundles are assembled by the Ask evidence packer under a token budget.
- **The gap:** narrow — the standing, always-current map file itself.
- **Disposition:** **partially built** (query-side landed Rounds 1–3; the single-artefact map **not yet dispositioned**).

---

**Reading the map honestly:** of the fifteen capabilities, two are at parity (6, 8), six are partially built (2, 3, 9, 12, 13, 15), three are owner-gated rather than technically blocked (1, 7, 10), one is rejected by design (14), and three stand honestly open with no disposition anywhere yet (4, 5, 11). The pattern matters more than any single row: **our remaining distance to "world's best" is mostly not unknown technology — it is gated decisions, one retrieval-quality problem (supersession), and evaluation depth.** The technology survey is done; the frontier now is rulings and depth, not breadth.
